⚠️ North Carolina's Revenue Fragmentation — The Strategic Problem

Every transit system in this benchmark receives government support — that is normal and expected for public transit. North Carolina's problem is not that its operators receive subsidies; it is that dozens of independent operators each run an identically thin non-fare revenue model with no statewide coordination, and government funding is fragmented across state appropriations and year-to-year federal formula grants. Three structural vulnerabilities define the current situation:

<5%
Non-fare commercial revenue at any NC operator — the lowest of all 9 systems benchmarked
$0
Statewide data licensing, unified loyalty program, or coordinated TOD program
$0
Dedicated, legally protected funding mechanism (no NC equivalent of LA Measure M or WMATA 2018 dedicated legislation)

Every peer has at least one strategic non-fare or dedicated funding program that NC lacks at scale. London has congestion pricing and ULEZ. Tokyo (JR East) earns Suica payment processing fees at 500,000+ retail locations. Singapore has diversified retail concessions and property income. LA Metro has voter-approved dedicated sales tax (Measure M/R: ~$2B/yr). Seoul earns T-money transaction fees at 100,000+ stores. CATS in Charlotte has a local transit sales tax — the only NC analog — but it is limited to one city and not coordinated statewide.

The gap is not primarily operational — it is structural and governance-driven. North Carolina needs both a statewide dedicated funding mechanism (the Measure M equivalent) and a parallel non-fare diversification program coordinated through NCDOT PTD. The benchmarks below demonstrate the playbook. Sources: NCDOT PTD · NCDOT UPTAS · CATS Transit Funding Report

NCDOT & NC Transit Revenue Opportunities — Lessons From Every Benchmarked Peer
💳
Statewide Contactless Payment Ecosystem
Modeled on: Tokyo Suica · Seoul T-money · GoTriangle GoPass/UMO pilot
Deploy contactless EMV across all major NC operators (GoRaleigh, GoTriangle, CATS, GoDurham, GoCary, Greensboro Transit), coordinated through NCDOT PTD. Expand the GoTriangle GoPass/UMO platform statewide. Over time, establish a North Carolina Transit Card accepted at park-and-ride vending, station retail, and partner retailers — generating transaction processing fees on every non-fare purchase. Tokyo's Suica earns hundreds of millions annually from retail transaction fees alone. Sources: GoTriangle GoPass/UMO · Tokyo Suica
Potential: $10–20M/yr (conservative); $50M+ at full statewide maturity
📊
NC Transit Open Data Portal + Data Licensing
Modeled on: Singapore LTA DataMall · TfL Open APIs
NCDOT PTD establishes a unified, open API platform aggregating GTFS-RT from all NC state-funded operators. Licensing this aggregated, anonymized mobility data to urban planners, real estate developers, logistics companies, and NC universities creates a recurring revenue stream from an asset NC already generates. Requires one foundational investment: GTFS-RT standardization mandate and API platform buildout. Sources: Singapore DataMall · FTA GTFS-RT
Potential: $3–10M/yr in data licensing fees (conservative)
🅿️
Dynamic Parking + EV Charging Revenue
Modeled on: WMATA Joint Development · MTA Real Estate
NC transit operators collectively operate major park-and-ride networks at CATS, GoTriangle (near RDU Airport), GoRaleigh, and other facilities. Implementing dynamic pricing (peak/off-peak rates), expanding EV charging with revenue-generating chargers, and developing transit-oriented commercial uses above surface lots would generate significant non-fare revenue from existing assets — coordinated statewide through NCDOT PTD.
Potential: $15–30M/yr statewide from dynamic pricing + EV charging
🎯
Statewide Rider Loyalty Program + Targeted Advertising
Modeled on: Tokyo JR East Lumine · Seoul Metro advertising
A statewide loyalty program built on contactless fare data (once unified EMV is deployed) would give NCDOT PTD a first-party data asset to offer targeted advertising to retail and hospitality partners across NC. Riders earn points redeemable at station retail, partner restaurants, or transit upgrades across all participating operators. Advertisers pay premium rates for targeted access to NC's high-growth urban commuter demographic.
Potential: $10–30M/yr in loyalty-driven advertising revenue at scale
🏗️
Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) — Statewide
Modeled on: WMATA Joint Development · JR East Real Estate
NC transit hubs represent significant underutilized real estate potential: Raleigh Union Station (GoRaleigh), Charlotte Center City Transit Station (CATS), Durham Station (GoDurham), and GoTriangle park-and-rides near RDU Airport. An NCDOT Joint Development Office — modeled on WMATA's 20+ project program — would coordinate joint development agreements generating upfront payments, ground lease income, and increased ridership. Sources: GoRaleigh · CATS
Potential: $30–80M/yr statewide in ground lease + development fees
🚌
NC Statewide Transit Revenue Coordination Act
Modeled on: LA Metro Measure M · London Congestion Charge · WMATA 2018 Dedicated Funding
North Carolina has no statewide equivalent of LA's dedicated sales tax, NYC's congestion pricing, or London's ULEZ charge. Advocating for a dedicated NC transit funding mechanism — a regional sales tax in major metros, VMT fee, or allocation of I-77/I-540 toll revenue to transit — would transform NC's financial resilience from year-to-year uncertainty to reliable long-term capital and operating funding. Sources: LA Measure M · NC I-77 Express Lanes
Potential: $200–500M+/yr if NC adopts dedicated funding model

NC Transit Revenue Diversification Roadmap — NCDOT PTD Coordination Framework
Near-Term — 0 to 18 Months

Foundation & Coordination

  • Mandate statewide GTFS-RT publication for all NCDOT-funded operators — foundational data infrastructure
  • Launch NCDOT NC Transit Open Data Portal (aggregate all operator feeds)
  • Establish statewide contactless payment standards committee (NCDOT PTD + all major operators)
  • Expand GoTriangle GoPass/UMO model to cover GoCary, GoDurham — Triangle consolidation first
  • Launch dynamic parking pricing at top 5–10 park-and-rides (CATS, GoTriangle near RDU, GoRaleigh)
  • Digitize and coordinate advertising inventory across CATS, GoTriangle, GoRaleigh stations
Mid-Term — 18 to 36 Months

Platform Development

  • Deploy unified contactless EMV across all major NC operators (coordinated by NCDOT PTD)
  • Launch NC Transit Rider Loyalty Program using statewide contactless fare data
  • Establish NCDOT Data Marketplace — begin licensing anonymized statewide mobility data
  • Create NCDOT Joint Development Office; sign first TOD agreements (targets: Raleigh Union Station, Charlotte Center City Transit Station, Durham Station)
  • Launch targeted advertising platform using NC statewide loyalty first-party data
  • Expand GoPass/UMO statewide — Charlotte (CATS), Greensboro Transit, GoRaleigh all on single platform
Long-Term — 36+ Months

Structural Transformation

  • Advocate for NC Statewide Transit Revenue Coordination Act — regional sales tax (Charlotte, Triangle, Greensboro) or VMT fee, modeled on LA Measure M
  • Pursue I-77/I-540 toll revenue allocation to transit capital — congestion pricing pilot with transit benefit
  • Scale NC Transit Card to retail acceptance beyond transit (convenience stores, parking, vending) statewide
  • Develop 5+ TOD projects across NC generating recurring ground lease income
  • Pursue federal grants (FTA 5339, RAISE, Infrastructure Law) tied to NCDOT PTD technology standardization program
  • Target: reduce combined NC operator government dependency from ~75% toward 60–65% through coordinated non-fare revenue diversification

Sources & Data Notes — All 9 Systems

Benchmark rating disclaimer: All revenue scores and fare recovery ratios are internal benchmark ratings produced for NCDOT benchmarking research purposes and are not independently validated industry ratings. NCDOT figures marked [TO BE VERIFIED] require confirmation from NCDOT UPTAS reports and individual operator financials before external citation. Peer system figures should be verified against each agency's most recent audited annual report before external citation.

Singapore LTA: Fare recovery (~55%) reflects operating cost coverage by passenger fares when full government capital commitment is counted — the government (via NRFF) owns all rail assets and (via BCM) absorbs all bus revenue risk, representing ~20% structural capital/ownership support. The 70% figure cited in some sources reflects operating-cost-only coverage under a model where government capital is already excluded. Non-fare revenue (~25%) includes retail concessions, advertising, tourist passes, and property income. LTA DataMall is primarily open/freemium data — direct licensing revenue is modest. Source: LTA Annual Report; Land Transport Masterplan 2040; NRFF/BCM public documentation.

SBB / Zürich: SBB passenger division fare recovery is approximately 38% of passenger segment operating costs. SBB receives substantial federal and cantonal government contributions (~32% of total system cost) under the Leistungsvereinbarung framework, covering infrastructure maintenance, network renewal, and subsidized regional passenger services. Total SBB revenue ~CHF 9–10B; additional revenue comes from freight (SBB Cargo), real estate (800+ commercial spaces), and international passes. Source: SBB Annual Report; Swiss Federal Council Leistungsvereinbarung 2021–2024.

London TfL: Non-fare revenue (~42%) — Congestion Charge (~£280M), ULEZ (~£200M), advertising, and property — exceeds fare revenue share, making TfL the most commercially diversified Western transit authority. All figures are approximate and vary year-on-year; verify against TfL Annual Report 2023/24. The £130M open data economic value estimate is per the 2017 Deloitte/TfL independent study and has not been updated since. Government grant (~23% of operating costs) covers the structural remaining gap. TfL contactless: buses from July 2012; Tube/rail from September 2014. Source: TfL Annual Report 2023/24; Deloitte/TfL 2017 open data study.

Tokyo JR East: JR East is a privatized listed company (fully privatized from JNR in 1987; government stake fully divested by 2002). It earns no operating subsidy; ~2% of total cost reflects minimal legacy infrastructure grants. Non-fare revenue (~40%) includes Suica payment processing fees (500K+ retail merchants), Lumine/NewDays/ecute station retail chains, Mets Hotels, real estate, and IT system licensing. Revenue figures are from COVID-era reporting — current revenue has recovered and may be higher. Suica 90M+ cards in circulation. Source: JR East Annual Report / Integrated Report; JR East IR materials.

Seoul Metro: Seoul Metro runs a structural operating deficit covered by the Seoul Metropolitan Government (~35% operating subsidy). Fares are kept low as a deliberate social policy, creating a persistent gap. Non-fare revenue (~15%) includes T-money payment ecosystem fees, station retail concessions, and digital advertising. T-money is accepted at 100,000+ retail locations city-wide, generating fee income independent of fare levels. Source: Seoul Metro Annual Report; Seoul Metropolitan Government budget documents.

MTA New York: MTA is ~45% dependent on NY State dedicated taxes (payroll mobility tax, petroleum business tax), NYC tax levy, and federal formula grants — all structural and recurring. OMNY fare cap is $34/week (not per day); weekly cap enables unlimited trips after reaching the threshold. NYC Congestion Pricing launched January 2025 in modified form; projected toll revenue ~$500–600M/yr supporting $1B+ in bonded capital capacity. Source: MTA FY2024 Budget; MTA Congestion Pricing Program documentation; MTA OMNY program materials.

WMATA (DC Metro): WMATA depends on ~60% government funding from DC/MD/VA jurisdictional contributions and federal grants. The 2018 dedicated funding legislation provides $500M+/yr in capital funding — a structural improvement over prior year-to-year uncertainty, but still subject to tri-jurisdictional political consensus annually. Joint Development program (20+ TOD projects) generates ground lease income. SmarTrip is interoperable across 15+ regional agencies. Source: WMATA FY2024 Budget; WMATA Compact; 2018 dedicated funding legislation documentation.

LA Metro: Fare recovery (~18%) is as cited in this benchmark; verify against LA Metro FY2023/24 audited financial statements before external use. The dedicated sales tax (Measure M, 2016 + Measure R, 2008) generates ~$2B/yr and is legally voter-locked, making it the most structurally stable US transit funding mechanism benchmarked. Express Lanes toll revenue is a growing supplemental stream. Source: LA Metro FY2024 Adopted Budget; Measure M/R ballot documentation.

NCDOT (NC Transit): Revenue breakdown for NC operators (combined estimate, all figures [TO BE VERIFIED via NCDOT UPTAS and individual operator reports]): Fare (~20–25%) + NCDOT PTD state grants/federal FTA formula grants (~70–75%) + Parking/Advertising/Other (~5%) = 100% of operating costs. No statewide coordination of non-fare commercial revenue exists. CATS (Charlotte) has a local transit sales tax (~$60M/yr estimate, [VERIFY]) — the only NC analog to a dedicated funding mechanism. GoTriangle's GoPass/UMO pilot generates modest technology fee income. Statewide data licensing potential is estimated at $3–10M/yr at conservative scale. Non-fare diversification reduces subsidy dependence — it does not directly change the fare recovery ratio. Source: NCDOT PTD (ncdot.gov); NCDOT UPTAS (connect.ncdot.gov); GoTriangle (gotriangle.org); CATS (catsride.org); GoRaleigh (goraleigh.org); FTA formula grant documentation.
