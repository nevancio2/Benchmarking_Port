The Problem IBM Solves — Direct Store Delivery & Inventory Minimization

What is DSD — and Why It Is One of the Hardest Supply Chain Problems

Direct Store Delivery (DSD) is a supply chain model used by snack food, beverage, and perishable goods companies where products go directly from manufacturing to retail shelves — without passing through a centralized distribution warehouse. Delivery drivers are simultaneously truck drivers, shelf stockers, inventory managers, and expired-product removers.

The result is a highly fragmented inventory landscape: thousands of individual delivery trucks and retail backrooms each holding small quantities of product, with no centralized visibility into where everything is, what is about to expire, or which locations are about to run out. Inventory space is always at a premium — every square foot of truck space and retail backroom costs money.

IBM's supply chain platform solves this by providing real-time visibility across every SKU at every location, automatically rebalancing inventory across routes to prevent overstocking in one place and stockouts in another — minimizing the total inventory space required across the entire system while ensuring shelves never go empty.

IBM Sterling Order Management (OMS)

Real-time visibility across every SKU in every truck, warehouse, and retail location. Automatically rebalances inventory across routes — the right product in the right place at the right time, minimizing total system inventory.

IBM ILOG CPLEX Optimization

IBM's industry-leading mathematical optimization engine. Applied to delivery routing: optimizes which driver delivers which products to which stores in which sequence — fewer miles, less fuel, smaller safety stock buffers needed.

IBM Planning Analytics (TM1)

Demand forecasting at the individual SKU and store level. Eliminates overproduction by aligning manufacturing output precisely to expected demand — fewer units produced means less space needed across the entire supply chain.

IBM Watson Supply Chain Visibility

Real-time tracking of every delivery vehicle, shipment, and inventory unit. Alerts managers the moment a delivery is delayed, a store is running low, or a product is approaching expiration — reducing waste from spoilage.

Grupo Bimbo: World's Largest Baking Company, Unified by IBM

Grupo Bimbo is the world's largest baking company — operating 197 plants, 33 countries, and 3.4 million delivery points daily. Every one of those delivery points represents a store location receiving fresh baked goods that must arrive on time, at the right quantity, without waste. This is arguably the most complex supply chain logistics operation in the food industry.

IBM's Sterling supply chain platform enabled Grupo Bimbo to achieve real-time visibility across its entire global delivery network, reduce delivery route inefficiencies significantly, and manage inventory across millions of daily deliveries with a minimal warehouse footprint. Before IBM, Grupo Bimbo's operations were fragmented across dozens of regional systems — exactly analogous to NCDOT's fragmented transit operator landscape.

AB InBev (Anheuser-Busch) is a second published IBM supply chain case: IBM's AI-driven demand forecasting helped AB InBev reduce overproduction and minimize inventory carrying costs across hundreds of breweries and thousands of distributors globally.

NCDOT Direct Parallel

NCDOT's Resource Allocation Problem Is Grupo Bimbo's Inventory Problem

Just as Grupo Bimbo had 3.4 million delivery points across 33 countries with no unified visibility of where product was, NCDOT has ferries, buses, maintenance crews, and highway assets deployed across the state with no unified real-time view of resource utilization. The IBM solution is the same: real-time visibility, mathematical optimization, and demand-driven deployment.

  • Ferry service: IBM ILOG optimization determines the right number of ferry vessels on each route at each time of day based on real-time demand data — eliminating both over-capacity (idle vessels) and under-capacity (missed passengers)
  • Transit bus deployment: IBM demand forecasting aligned to events, weather, and commute patterns ensures the right number of buses on the right routes — reducing dead mileage and overtime labor costs
  • Highway maintenance crews: IBM route optimization deploys maintenance teams to the highest-priority locations first, minimizing response time and crew idle time
  • Equipment and parts inventory: IBM Sterling OMS tracks maintenance parts inventory across all NCDOT depots — ensuring the right parts are at the right facility before a scheduled maintenance event, eliminating delays caused by parts being at the wrong location

Right-Size the Ferry Fleet

IBM demand forecasting and route optimization determines optimal vessel deployment per route and time window — reducing idle vessel costs while ensuring capacity for peak periods like holiday weekends and coastal event traffic.

Optimize Transit Deployment

IBM's demand-driven deployment model — proven at Grupo Bimbo's 3.4M delivery points — scales directly to NCDOT's dozens of transit operators and hundreds of routes, reducing dead mileage and labor waste.

Unify Parts & Maintenance Inventory

IBM Sterling OMS gives NCDOT real-time visibility of maintenance parts inventory across all depots statewide — the same way it gives Grupo Bimbo real-time visibility of baked goods across millions of delivery points.
