What IBM Did

Veterans Benefits Administration Modernization

The Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) claims system was broadly considered one of the most complex modernization challenges in the federal government. Decades of accumulated systems, regional fragmentation, and mission-critical 24/7 service delivery created a situation where traditional modernization approaches were deemed too risky to attempt.

IBM successfully transformed the VA's claims processing through a combination of AI-driven intake automation and phased system integration. IBM deployed AI capabilities to automate the ingestion of benefits data into VA systems — a process the VA subsequently documented in a published AI Inventory submitted to oversight bodies. This was not a laboratory demonstration: the system directly determined Veteran service delivery and benefits eligibility processing.

IBM's Federal Government Solutions practice, which spans citizen services to national defense, provided the framework for this engagement. The VA simultaneously committed to a 5-Year IT Benefits Delivery Modernization Plan, delivered to Congress, which built on the IBM-supported foundation. Throughout all of this, the VA never took its claims system offline — Veteran benefits continued uninterrupted.

The Data Silo Problem IBM Solved

The Problem

Fragmented claims, benefits, and service delivery data lived in isolated systems across dozens of regional VA offices — each with its own data formats, processes, and access rules.

No unified statewide visibility existed. VA employees in one region could not see the full picture of a Veteran's case handled by another region.

The Constraint

Unlike a commercial system that could be taken offline for migration, the VA's claims system determines life-affecting benefits for millions of Veterans. Any modernization had to happen with full operational continuity — a constraint directly analogous to NCDOT's 24/7 ferry, highway, and transit operations.

IBM's Solution

IBM applied AI-driven automation to standardize data ingestion across regional systems, creating a unified benefits data layer without requiring individual regional systems to be replaced. This created an integrated visibility layer over the top of existing infrastructure — a model NCDOT can directly apply.

NCDOT Relevance — Why This Case Matters to You
NCDOT Direct Parallel

NCDOT's Regional Fragmentation is the VA Problem — Applied to Transportation

NCDOT's challenge mirrors the VA's exactly: data from regional operators — CATS, GoTriangle, Wave Transit, GoDurham, Greensboro Transit — lives in isolated systems with no unified statewide visibility layer. IBM has already solved this at government scale.

  • No unified statewide transit data: Each regional operator runs independent systems, just as each VA regional office did
  • 24/7 constraint: NCDOT cannot take ferry operations, highway monitoring, or toll collection offline during modernization
  • Mission-critical parallel: Just as VA claims affect Veteran lives, NCDOT systems support daily commuters, freight movement, and emergency access

What NCDOT Can Take from This Case

The VA engagement proves that IBM can handle the specific government constraint NCDOT faces: you cannot stop operations to modernize them. IBM built a unified visibility layer on top of the existing VA regional systems — the same approach that applies to NCDOT's regional transit operators.

The path for NCDOT is to create a statewide transportation data visibility layer that draws from each regional operator's existing system — GoTriangle, CATS, GoRaleigh, Wave Transit — without requiring any operator to replace their current platform. IBM demonstrated this is exactly achievable at the VA's scale and complexity.

  • Deploy IBM's integration layer to pull data from each regional NCDOT operator in standardized format
  • Create a unified statewide transit visibility dashboard for NCDOT planners and executives
  • Extend the same pattern to ferries, toll systems, and highway monitoring data
  • Foundation is then ready for AI-driven analytics without any regional system having to be rebuilt
