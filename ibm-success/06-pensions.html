What IBM Did

Government Workforce Systems Modernization — Without Disrupting Operations

Government agencies at every level — federal, state, and local — commonly struggle with pension, payroll, benefits, and workforce data trapped in completely separate systems. IBM has successfully modernized these as "complicated subsystems" — treating each (payroll, benefits, pensions, workforce scheduling) as a bounded unit that can be modernized independently, without replacing the primary business process that employees and managers depend on daily.

IBM has been directly and actively involved in federal HR and benefits modernization at the highest levels. IBM filed a formal protest with the GAO over the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) HR IT system modernization procurement — underscoring that IBM is not a peripheral player in this space, but a primary competitor actively shaping federal workforce systems strategy.

IBM also publishes guidance on AI-enabled workforce transformation in government HR, specifically addressing the change management dimension: how to bring employees along through modernization so that workforce uncertainty does not become operational disruption. This human-centered approach is directly relevant to NCDOT's frontline workforce — ferry crews, highway maintenance teams, and transit drivers who depend on reliable payroll and scheduling systems.

NCDOT Direct Parallel

NCDOT Has Employee Data Silos Preventing Operational Optimization and Cost Visibility

NCDOT's ferry crews, highway maintenance teams, and transit drivers are its most significant operational cost center. Without a unified workforce data layer, NCDOT cannot answer basic operational questions that drive cost efficiency and service quality:

  • What is the total labor cost per ferry route after accounting for crew scheduling, overtime, and benefits?
  • Which vehicles in the NCDOT fleet are most likely to fail in the next 90 days based on driver behavior and maintenance history?
  • Across the entire NCDOT transit network, which routes have the highest labor cost per rider served?

Predictive Maintenance

IBM correlates driver behavior data (acceleration, braking, idle time) with vehicle maintenance histories and scheduled service records to predict which vehicles will need unplanned maintenance — before they fail.

Applied to NCDOT's ferry fleet, highway vehicles, and transit buses, this reduces emergency repair costs and prevents service disruptions.

Workforce Optimization

IBM's workforce intelligence layer enables NCDOT to optimize crew and driver scheduling across ferry routes, highway maintenance districts, and transit corridors — reducing overtime costs while maintaining service levels.

This is especially valuable for NCDOT's ferry division, where crew scheduling is directly tied to vessel availability and weather constraints.

Route-Level Cost Visibility

IBM creates real-time labor cost visibility by route and mode — a capability NCDOT currently lacks entirely. This data is essential for federal grant applications, state budget justifications, and negotiations with regional transit operators about service standards and funding formulas.
