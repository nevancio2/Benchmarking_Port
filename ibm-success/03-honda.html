What IBM Did

Honda: AI-Driven Integration Across Fragmented Operational Data

IBM's engagement with Honda addressed two related but distinct silo problems. First, Honda's operational documentation — technical specifications, maintenance records, parts data — existed in separate silos with no unified knowledge layer. IBM deployed AI-driven knowledge extraction to unify this documentation, dramatically reducing the time required to model and process it.

Second, Honda Canada explored mainframe integration to modernize the Honda Dealer Portal for its sales organization — delivering trusted, unified data across Salesforce for what IBM calls "agentic enterprises": organizations using AI agents that need reliable, real-time data to act autonomously on behalf of users.

IBM maintains a dedicated Automotive Industry IT Solutions practice with published Honda case work, providing a transparent, reference-able engagement for clients like NCDOT who want to understand the methodology in detail.

The Data Silo Problem IBM Solved

Honda's Silo Challenge

Honda's documentation, dealer portal data, and sales information lived in completely separate systems. Knowledge was not flowing between them — meaning engineers couldn't leverage documentation insights for sales support, and sales teams couldn't surface relevant technical data for dealers in real time.

The key IBM innovation at Honda was boundary-focused integration: rather than attempting to unify all Honda data everywhere, IBM identified precisely which integration boundary mattered most — documentation-to-operations — and attacked that boundary first.

The Strategic Lens for NCDOT

The Honda case teaches NCDOT a critical prioritization discipline that the IRS and VA cases do not: not all silos are equally important to address first.

  • Which NCDOT silo, if unified, creates the most downstream value?
  • Should modernization focus on one modal silo (e.g., ferries only)?
  • Or address the integration boundary between ferries, highways, transit, and tolls to enable cross-modal revenue insights?

IBM's Honda methodology gives NCDOT the framework to answer these questions before committing to any technology investment.

NCDOT Relevance — Why This Case Matters to You
NCDOT Direct Parallel

NCDOT's Maintenance Documentation is Honda's Documentation Problem

NCDOT maintains thousands of vehicles, vessels, and highway infrastructure assets. Maintenance records, inspection data, parts histories, and work orders exist across fragmented systems — exactly the documentation silo IBM solved at Honda. The 67% time reduction IBM achieved is directly applicable.

  • Ferry fleet maintenance: Vessel inspection records, engine histories, crew certifications — all currently siloed
  • Highway maintenance equipment: Fleet vehicle histories, repair records, and fuel costs in separate systems
  • Transit assets: Bus maintenance records managed independently by each regional operator

The Strategic Silo Prioritization Question for NCDOT

The most important NCDOT takeaway from Honda is not the 67% metric — it is the question IBM forces its clients to answer before implementation begins: which silos do you attack first, and why?

IBM's Honda engagement showed that the right answer is often not the most obvious one. For NCDOT, the highest-value integration boundary may not be between its largest systems — it may be between a specific operational data source (e.g., ferry GPS data) and a financial data source (e.g., toll revenue) that, when connected, unlock a completely new revenue opportunity.

IBM's role is to guide NCDOT through this prioritization before any technology spending begins — ensuring every dollar of modernization investment addresses the boundary that creates the most operational and revenue value.
