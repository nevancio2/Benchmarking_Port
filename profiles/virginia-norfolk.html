Dimension 1 — Throughput & Scale

3.5 Million TEUs — Strong Growth, Clear Upward Trajectory

The Port of Virginia handled 3,503,129 TEUs in FY2024 (year ended June 30, 2024), positioning it as the 3rd-largest East Coast container port by TEU volume, behind NY/NJ (~9.0M TEUs) and Savannah (~5.5–5.9M TEUs) and ahead of Charleston (~2.5M TEUs). Virginia's TEU trajectory has been consistently upward, driven by the Gateway Investment Program infrastructure improvements, strong carrier relationships, and a competitive on-dock rail program.

As the ULCV transition accelerates — with the 55-foot channel allowing fully loaded mega-ships to call — Virginia's volume growth advantage over shallower-draft competitors is expected to widen. Virginia has been selected as the first U.S. East Coast port of call for CMA CGM's upgraded Chesapeake Bay Express ULCV service, a direct market confirmation of carrier confidence.

Score: 85/100

Third position in benchmark volume ranking, with a stronger growth trajectory than Charleston and a clear competitive pathway as the 55-foot channel is fully utilized.

Dimension 2 — Operational Efficiency (CPPI)

Top-Performing U.S. East Coast Port on the World Bank CPPI

The Port of Virginia is the top-performing U.S. East Coast port on the World Bank Container Port Performance Index (CPPI) across multiple editions. This is not a marginal advantage — Virginia's automation-first approach fundamentally separates it from every other port in this benchmark.

The Virginia International Gateway (VIG) in Portsmouth operates with automated stacking cranes (ASCs) — a technology deployed at relatively few North American terminals. ASCs replace rubber-tired gantry cranes with computer-controlled stacked container handling, reducing cycle time, labor cost per lift, and operational variability. The World Bank CPPI 2020–2024 publication explicitly identifies automation as a consistent predictor of higher moves-per-port-hour (MPPH) scores. VIG's ASC deployment is the primary driver of Virginia's CPPI leadership.

Score: 93/100

Highest efficiency score in benchmark. Combination of ASC automation at VIG, 55-foot channel enabling maximum ULCV productivity, on-dock rail at both major terminals, and consistent CPPI top-performer status. The 7-point deduction reflects NIT not yet fully automated and the ILA labor environment limiting further automation expansion.

Dimension 3 — Infrastructure & Physical Capacity

America's Most Modern Gateway — The Infrastructure Benchmark

Virginia's infrastructure portfolio is the strongest in this benchmark on nearly every dimension. The $450 million dredging program is bringing the Norfolk Harbor channel to 55 feet at mean low water — the deepest harbor on the U.S. East Coast upon completion (nearing completion as of January 2026). Channel widening was completed in February 2024, enabling two ultra-large container vessels to transit simultaneously — a capability no other East Coast port offers.

Channel Depth

55 feet (target, near completion) — deepest on the U.S. East Coast; enables fully loaded ULCVs above 18,000 TEU that no competing port can accept

STS Cranes

29 ship-to-shore cranes across VIG and NIT — all post-Panamax or super post-Panamax; 2nd largest fleet in benchmark behind Savannah (38)

Capital Program

$1.4B Gateway Investment Program — channel dredging, channel widening, new ULCV berth at NIT, ASC deployment at VIG, $83M rail infrastructure. Substantially complete, benefits being realized now.

Score: 98/100

Top infrastructure score in benchmark. The 55-foot channel, 29 STS cranes, ASC automation at VIG, dual-ULCV simultaneous transit capability, and $1.4B near-complete capital program represent a combination no other port in this benchmark can match.

Dimension 4 — Financial Performance & Revenue Model

$113M Operating Income — Strongest Single-Port Result in Benchmark

The Port of Virginia generated operating income of $113 million in FY2024 (year ended June 30, 2024) on total cargo volume of 3.5 million TEUs — the highest operating income of any single-purpose container port authority in this benchmark. VPA operates through Virginia International Terminals (VIT), its wholly owned subsidiary that runs all terminal operations, meaning VPA captures the full operational revenue stream rather than sharing it with a private lessee.

On-dock rail at both VIG and NIT, served by both CSX and Norfolk Southern, is the gold standard in port intermodal connectivity — containers move directly between ship and train without an intermediate truck dray. Virginia's $83 million rail upgrade (part of the Gateway Investment Program) is expanding this capacity further.

Virginia's 55-foot channel (deepest East Coast) creates an additional connectivity advantage: carriers deploying their largest vessels route them through ports that can physically accept them fully loaded. Virginia captures the highest-capacity vessel deployments and the premium trade lanes that use ULCVs.

Score: 90/100

Strongest hinterland reach in benchmark. On-dock rail at both major terminals, dual Class I railroad service, Virginia Inland Port, and ULCV vessel service premium combine for the top connectivity score. The 10-point deduction reflects Virginia's geographic hinterland being smaller than Savannah (broader Southeast footprint) and NY/NJ (largest U.S. consumer market).

Dimension 6 — Governance & Strategic Positioning

Single-Purpose State Authority With Proven Capital Execution

The Virginia Port Authority is a political subdivision of the Commonwealth of Virginia. The hybrid structure — VPA owns assets, VIT (100% VPA subsidiary) operates them — gives VPA the strategic and capital authority of a public body while capturing the full operational revenue of a terminal operator. Virginia's $1.4B Gateway Investment Program is the most ambitious and most complete capital program in this benchmark — clearly envisioned, comprehensively funded, and nearing completion.

"America's Most Modern Gateway" — Virginia's branding is grounded in verifiable infrastructure facts (ASCs, deepest channel, on-dock rail at two terminals) rather than mere marketing, and carrier behavior (CMA CGM ULCV service selection) validates it in the marketplace.

Score: 85/100

Single-purpose state authority with strong capital program execution, clear strategic vision, and demonstrated carrier confidence. The deduction reflects the need for continued investment to maintain the infrastructure lead and the complexity of managing both automated (VIG) and non-automated (NIT) operations simultaneously.
