Sources & Methodology Notes

Benchmark Scores: All scores (Technology, UX, Silo Integration, Revenue) are internal benchmark ratings produced by this NCDOT Benchmarking Research 2024 study. They are not independently validated industry ratings. Methodology weighs each dimension equally across pre/during/post-transit phases. NCDOT is a statewide coordinating DOT, not a single operator — its peer comparison against unified metro agencies (Singapore LTA, TfL, etc.) is intentional and reflects the coordination opportunity, with this caveat noted for credibility.

Ranking Note: Tokyo JR East (88/100) is ranked #4 and Seoul Metro (86/100) is ranked #5 in accordance with their overall scores. Rankings reflect scores at time of research.

Revenue Data: Budget and fare recovery figures are sourced from agency annual reports and public financial disclosures for the benchmarked period. Singapore LTA revenue reflects operating costs covered by fares and non-fare streams; government capital funding under the New Rail Financing Framework (NRFF) is provided separately. SBB (Zurich) receives federal infrastructure subsidies in addition to its commercial revenues. NCDOT figures are estimates pending verification via NCDOT UPTAS and individual operator reports. All figures should be verified against each agency's most recent audited financials.

Key Sources: WMATA FY2024 Adopted Budget; MTA FY2024 Budget; TfL Annual Report 2023/24; JR East Annual Report; Seoul Metro Annual Statistical Report; Singapore LTA Annual Report; SBB Annual Report; LA Metro FY2024 Adopted Budget; NCDOT Public Transportation Division (ncdot.gov); NCDOT UPTAS (connect.ncdot.gov). London open data value per 2017 Deloitte/TfL study.

Congestion Pricing (MTA): NYC congestion pricing is projected in the benchmark to generate $1B+ annually for capital investment capacity (via bonding). Toll revenue was projected at ~$500–600M annually. The program launched in modified form in January 2025 after a temporary pause.
